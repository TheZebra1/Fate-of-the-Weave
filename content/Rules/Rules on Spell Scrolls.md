# Spell Scrolls

> [!abstract] At the table
> **Use:** Check whether the spell is on your spell list. If it is, read the scroll and cast the spell using its normal casting time. You don’t provide Material components. The scroll is consumed when the casting finishes; an interrupted casting doesn’t consume it.
>
> **Non-spellcaster:** A character without access to the spell on a spell list generally can’t use a Spell Scroll. The clear 2024 exception is a **Thief Rogue at level 13**, whose *Use Magic Device* feature lets them use any Spell Scroll.
>
> **Make:** Use the **2024 Player’s Handbook** table below for the current crafting rules. The Kibbles method is an optional alternative; don’t combine the two crafting systems.

## Using a Spell Scroll

A Spell Scroll is a magic item that stores one spell. To use it, you must be able to read it and the spell must be on the spell list available to you. The 2024 Player’s Handbook equipment entry says **class’s spell list**; the 2024 Dungeon Master’s Guide item entry shortens this to **your spell list**. This note uses the class-associated spell list as the table ruling.

- You don’t need to have the spell prepared or known to use the scroll.
- Cast the spell using its **normal casting time**, range, duration, and other rules.
- You provide no **Material components**. Any Verbal or Somatic components the spell requires still apply.
- The scroll’s spell save DC and attack bonus are set by its level (see the table below), unless a feature says otherwise.
- The scroll crumbles to dust when the spell’s casting is completed. If the casting is interrupted, the scroll isn’t lost.
- Casting from a scroll doesn’t expend a spell slot. You still concentrate on the spell if it requires Concentration.

### If the Spell Is Too High-Level

If the spell is on your spell list but is higher level than you can normally cast, make an ability check using your spellcasting ability. The DC is **10 + the spell’s level**. On a failure, the spell disappears from the scroll without taking effect.

> [!example] Example
> A 3rd-level spell scroll used by a character who can normally cast only 2nd-level spells requires a spellcasting ability check against **DC 13**.

### Using Scrolls Without Spellcasting

- **Most non-spellcasters:** The Spell Scroll’s spell-list requirement prevents use; the general rule that lets literate creatures try to activate scrolls applies to scrolls that don’t have a more specific restriction.
- **2024 Thief Rogue, level 13:** *Use Magic Device* lets you use **any Spell Scroll**, using Intelligence as your spellcasting ability. Cantrips and 1st-level spells work reliably. For a higher-level spell, make an Intelligence (Arcana) check against **DC 10 + the spell’s level**. On a failure, the scroll disintegrates.
- **Other features:** If a feature explicitly lets you use a Spell Scroll or gives you the relevant spell list, follow that feature.

> [!info] Table ruling for this campaign
> The 2024 DMG’s Spell Scroll entry says “your spell list,” while the 2024 PHB equipment entry says “your class’s spell list.” Until the table chooses otherwise, use the narrower, class-associated-list reading. A character with no spellcasting feature doesn’t gain general Spell Scroll access just from being able to read.

### Copying a Scroll into a Spellbook

A Wizard can copy a Wizard spell from a Spell Scroll into their spellbook by succeeding on an **Intelligence (Arcana) check** with a DC of **10 + the spell’s level**. On a success, the spell is copied. The scroll is destroyed whether the check succeeds or fails.

## Scribing a Spell Scroll — 2024 Rules

A spellcaster can scribe a scroll if they meet the prerequisites below. These are the current core crafting rules.

### Prerequisites

- Proficiency in **Arcana** *or* **Calligrapher’s Supplies**.
- The spell must be **prepared on each day** of inscription.
- Any Material components required by the spell must be available. If the spell consumes them, they are consumed when the scroll is completed.
- A scribed scroll uses the creator’s spell save DC and spell attack bonus.
- A scribed cantrip works as if its caster were the creator’s level.

Each workday is **8 hours**. Days need not be consecutive.

| Spell level | Workdays | Scribing cost |
|---:|---:|---:|
| Cantrip | 1 | 15 gp |
| 1st | 1 | 25 gp |
| 2nd | 3 | 100 gp |
| 3rd | 5 | 150 gp |
| 4th | 10 | 1,000 gp |
| 5th | 25 | 1,500 gp |
| 6th | 40 | 10,000 gp |
| 7th | 50 | 12,500 gp |
| 8th | 60 | 15,000 gp |
| 9th | 120 | 50,000 gp |

> [!tip] Scroll value
> The 2024 DMG sets a Spell Scroll’s value at **twice its scribing cost**. This is a suggested item value, not a guarantee that a buyer or seller is available.

## Optional Crafting System — Kibbles’ Guide

> [!warning] Choose one crafting method
> This is the campaign’s optional Kibbles-based crafting procedure, transcribed from [[Crafting and Harvesting.pdf#page=96&selection=4,0,4,14|Crafting and Harvesting]]. Use it **instead of** the 2024 scribing table above. Its materials, checks, times, and costs are a separate system.

To scribe a scroll using this method, you must:

- Have proficiency in **Arcana** or with **Calligrapher’s Tools**.
- Have the spell prepared on each day of inscription.
- Have any Material components required by the spell. Components consumed by the spell are consumed only when the scroll is completed.
- Work no more than **8 hours per day**. The listed hours may be split across days.
- Pay the listed gold infusion cost and provide the listed materials.
- Make the listed number of crafting checks against the listed DC. The scroll uses your spell save DC and spell attack bonus. A cantrip scroll works as if its caster were your level.
