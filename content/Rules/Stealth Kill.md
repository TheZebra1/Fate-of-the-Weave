# Stealth Kill

> [!abstract] Rule summary
> An attacker who remains hidden can attempt one quiet attack against an unaware target. If the attack hits and deals enough damage to reduce the target to 0 Hit Points, the target dies without starting combat.

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the target is unaware.** The attacker must be hidden from the target and any nearby creatures who could notice the approach. The DM determines which creatures are close enough to be potential witnesses.
2. **Check Stealth.** The attacker makes one Dexterity (Stealth) check and compares the result with the Passive Perception of the target and each potential witness. The check must succeed against all of them for the attacker to approach unnoticed.
3. **Resolve the attack.**
   - **Stealth succeeds against everyone:** The attacker makes one attack before initiative is rolled. If the target can’t see the attacker, the attack has Advantage as normal for an unseen attacker.
   - **Stealth fails against anyone:** That creature notices the attacker before the attack. Do not make the attack; roll initiative.
4. **Determine the outcome.**
   - **Hit deals enough damage:** If the attack’s damage reduces the target to 0 Hit Points, the target is killed quietly. Do not roll initiative unless a witness notices the attack.
   - **Hit deals too little damage:** Apply the damage normally, then roll initiative. The attacker has spent their Action for the first round.
   - **Attack misses:** The target is alerted; roll initiative. The attacker has spent their Action for the first round.

## Damage Threshold

The attack must deal damage equal to or greater than the target’s current Hit Points **plus any Temporary Hit Points**. Include all damage dice and modifiers, then apply the target’s resistances, vulnerabilities, and immunities.

A successful Stealth check does not make the attack hit automatically. Resolve the attack roll and damage normally.

## Witnesses and Noise

A witness who succeeds on the Stealth check remains unaware of the attacker’s approach, but may still hear or see the attack itself. The DM determines whether the weapon, distance, surroundings, or other circumstances alert a witness.

If no one notices the attack, a later discovery of the body can still raise an alarm.

## Example

*Lira, a 4th-level rogue, sneaks up on a lone bandit with 10 Hit Points and no Temporary Hit Points. She beats the bandit’s Passive Perception, then makes her attack with Advantage. If she hits and deals at least 10 damage, the bandit dies quietly. If she hits but deals less than 10 damage—or misses—resolve the attack, then roll initiative; Lira has spent her Action for the first round.*
