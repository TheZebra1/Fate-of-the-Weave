## 01 · Potions
A creature can drink a potion as an **Action** or a **Bonus Action**.

- **Action:** The potion restores the maximum possible amount of healing.
- **Bonus Action:** Roll the potion's healing dice normally.

## 02 · Critical Hits
On a critical hit, roll the attack's damage dice as normal, then add a second set of those dice at their maximum value. Add damage modifiers only once.

A creature hit by a critical hit gains **1 level of Exhaustion**.

## 03 · Sleep Deprivation
A creature that completes no Long Rest during a 24-hour period gains **1 level of Exhaustion** at the end of that period.

## 04 · Water Requirements
A Small or Medium creature requires **1 gallon of water per day**, or **1.5 gallons in hot weather**. A standard [[waterskin-xphb|waterskin]] holds **0.5 gallons**.

- **Some water, but half or less of the daily requirement:** Make a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** at the end of the day. On a failure, gain 1 level of Exhaustion.
- **No water:** Gain 1 level of Exhaustion at the end of the day without a saving throw.
- The DM determines daily water requirements for creatures of other sizes.
- Exhaustion caused by dehydration can’t be reduced until the creature drinks its full daily water requirement. Normal Exhaustion recovery then applies.

## 05 · Spell Scrolls

## 06 · Flanking
When you make a melee attack, gain **+1 to the attack roll for each allied creature within 5 feet of the target and directly opposite you**. Multiple qualifying allies can grant multiple bonuses.

## 07 · Armor and Weapon Durability
Armor can be damaged by critical hits, and weapons can be damaged by critical fumbles. See [[Armor-Weapon Durability System]] for durability, breakage, and repairs.

## 08 · Stealth Kill
A hidden attacker can kill an unaware target with one attack if the attack hits and its damage is sufficient to reduce the target to 0 Hit Points. See [[Stealth Kill]] for the full procedure.

## 09 · Dashing Exhaustion
When a creature takes the Dash action on its turn, unless the action is granted by a spell, feature, or magic item, it must make a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw**. On a failure, it gains **1 temporary level of Exhaustion**.

- Track this Exhaustion separately from normal Exhaustion. It ends when the current initiative ends.
- A creature can’t die from this temporary Exhaustion. If it reaches level 6, it falls unconscious until the current initiative ends.

## 10 · Monster Compendium
After the party defeats a creature, its stat block can be requested from the DM at any later time.

## 11 · Wild Shape Reversion
A druid can end Wild Shape and return to their normal form without using an action.

## 12 · Stacking Advantage and Disadvantage
Count each source of Advantage and Disadvantage; cancel one source of each against one another.

- **No net sources:** Roll normally.
- **One net source:** Roll with Advantage or Disadvantage as appropriate.
- **Two or more net sources:** Roll with Advantage or Disadvantage, then add or subtract a modifier equal to the number of net sources.

## 13 · Soft Critical Hits
If an attack roll is **greater than twice the target’s AC**, and the roll is not a natural 20, the attack is a **soft critical hit**.

Maximize the attack’s damage dice once; do not roll or add a second set of dice. Add damage modifiers only once. A soft critical hit doesn’t count as a critical hit for other rules or features.

## 14 · Exact-AC Hits
An attack roll that **exactly equals** the target’s AC is a hit, but deals **half damage**, rounded down. Resolve damage normally, then halve the final damage.

A natural 1 or natural 20 takes precedence over this rule.

## 15 · Resurrection Exhaustion
Whenever a creature returns to life after dying, it gains **1 level of Exhaustion**. This replaces the 2014 rule that being raised from the dead reduces Exhaustion by 1.

Exhaustion gained this way follows the normal rules for recovery. Finishing a Long Rest reduces it by 1 level, and any effect that removes Exhaustion can remove it.

### Level 6 Exception
A creature whose Exhaustion reaches level 6 as a result of returning to life remains alive. It suffers all other effects of its Exhaustion level, including the effects of lower levels. Its Exhaustion can’t exceed level 6.

This exception doesn’t prevent death from damage or any other effect that causes death.
