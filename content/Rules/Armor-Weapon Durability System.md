# Armor and Weapon Durability

> [!abstract] At a glance
> **Armor:** A critical hit damages one defensive item. Armor retains its AC until durability reaches 0; then it loses AC.
>
> **Weapons:** A natural 1 on a weapon attack damages the weapon. A weapon at 0 durability accumulates an attack penalty and can eventually break.

## Armor Durability

When a creature wearing armor is hit by a critical hit, the armor takes durability damage equal to the damage dealt by that attack, after resistance, vulnerability, and other damage adjustments but before Temporary Hit Points are lost.

A shield takes damage only if the DM determines it intercepted or blocked the attack. A critical hit damages only one defensive item.

- Armor keeps its normal AC until its durability reaches 0.
- When armor is reduced to 0 durability, it loses **1 AC**.
- While at 0 durability, each further critical hit against the wearer reduces its AC by another **1**, to a maximum penalty of **−2 AC**.
- A shield follows the same procedure, reducing its **+2 AC bonus** by 1 each time, to a minimum bonus of 0.
- Lost AC is restored when the item is fully repaired.

### Repairing Armor

Armor can be repaired at a smithy or by a creature proficient with the relevant tools. Use Smith’s Tools for metal armor and shields; use Leatherworker’s Tools for leather and hide armor.

- **Repair rate:** 10 durability per hour.
- **Repair cost:** The table lists the cost to fully repair an item from 0 durability. Partial repairs cost a proportional amount, rounded up to the nearest silver piece.
- **Full repair:** Restores the item to maximum durability and restores any lost AC.

### Armor Example

A critical hit deals 25 damage to a fighter wearing chain mail with 50 durability. The chain mail falls to 25 durability and keeps its normal AC. A later critical hit reduces it to 0 durability, so it loses 1 AC. Repairing it from 0 to 30 durability takes 3 hours and costs 18 gp. Once fully repaired to 50 durability, it regains the lost AC.

## Weapon Durability

When a creature rolls a natural 1 on an attack roll made with a weapon, that weapon takes durability damage equal to the weapon’s damage dice, rolled without modifiers. Use the weapon damage dice for the attack as made. A weapon with no damage die takes 1 durability damage.

- A weapon retains its normal attack bonus until its durability reaches 0.
- When a weapon is reduced to 0 durability, it takes a **−1 penalty to attack rolls**.
- Each further natural 1 with that weapon while it remains at 0 durability increases the penalty by **−1**.
- If the penalty reaches **−4**, the weapon is broken and can’t be used.
- A broken weapon must be fully repaired before it can be used again. A full repair restores its maximum durability and removes all penalties.

### Repairing Weapons

Weapons can be repaired at a smithy or by a creature proficient with the appropriate tools. The table lists the usual tool proficiency; the DM determines whether a different tool is suitable for a particular weapon.

- **Repair rate:** 5 durability per hour.
- **Repair cost:** The table lists the cost to fully repair an item from 0 durability. Partial repairs cost a proportional amount, rounded up to the nearest silver piece.
- **Full repair:** Restores the weapon to maximum durability and removes all attack penalties.

### Weapon Example

A rogue’s dagger has 10 durability. On a natural 1, the rogue rolls the dagger’s 1d4 damage die and subtracts the result from its durability. If the dagger later reaches 0 durability, it takes a −1 penalty to attack rolls. Further natural 1s while it remains at 0 increase the penalty; at −4, the dagger is broken.

## Magic Weapons

- **Common and Uncommon:** Follow the same durability rules as nonmagical weapons.
- **Rare, Very Rare, and Legendary:** Immune to durability damage from ordinary weapon fumbles. Extraordinary magic or story events can still damage them at the DM’s discretion.
