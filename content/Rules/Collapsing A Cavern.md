### **Framework for Collapsing a Cavern**
[[collapsing-roof-xdmg|Collapsing Roof]]
Collapsing a cavern can be a dramatic and creative solution for players. This framework outlines how to structure such an event, making it plausible, challenging, and fair while allowing for player ingenuity.

#### **Step 1: Identifying Key Weak Points**
Players must locate areas in the cavern where their actions could trigger a collapse.  
- **Skill Checks to Identify Weak Points**:  
  - **Investigation DC 18:** Spots a large unstable stalactite, cracked ceiling section, or other weak structures.  
  - **Nature DC 18**: Determines areas where the rock is brittle or has been weakened by erosion.
  - **Arcana DC 18**: Detects residual magical energy or unnatural instability that might influence the cavern's structure.  

#### **Step 2: Triggering the Collapse**
Once weak points are identified, players must execute a plan to destabilize them. Possible methods include:  

- **Explosives or Spells**:  
  - Area-of-effect spells like *Fireball*, *Shatter*, or *Earth Tremor* can be effective if targeted precisely.  
  - **Skill Checks to Aim**: Require a DC 14 Spellcasting ability check or an Attack Roll to target the weak point.  

- **Physical Actions**:  
  - Players could use **Mining Tools** (DC 15 Athletics) or detonate existing explosives (e.g., alchemist’s fire) near weak areas.  
  - **Ranged Attacks**: Weapons like crossbows or thrown javelins might dislodge precarious stalactites or rubble.  

- **Distraction or Sabotage**:  
  - Players could lure creatures or create disturbances that naturally destabilize the weak points. For example, large enemies might inadvertently cause structural damage.  

#### **Step 3: Timing and Risk**
A collapse shouldn't happen instantly. Build tension by requiring multiple steps or rounds to complete:  
- **Rounds to Complete**: Players need 3-4 successful actions targeting weak points before the collapse begins.  
- **Hazards During Preparation**: If enemies or environmental factors interfere, players might face combat or complications as they work.  

#### **Step 4: Escaping the Collapse**
Once triggered, everyone in the cavern is at risk. Make escape its own challenge:  
- **Countdown**: Announce the collapse with tremors, falling debris, and environmental chaos. Give 3-5 rounds for players to escape.  

- **Skill Checks to Escape**:  
  - **Athletics (DC 15)**: Navigate uneven terrain or climb obstacles.  
  - **Acrobatics (DC 14)**: Avoid falling debris or dart through narrow gaps.  
  - **Constitution (DC 12)**: Resist exhaustion from dust, heat, and physical exertion.  

- **Danger Zones**: Characters who linger in certain areas risk falling rocks (e.g., 2d10 bludgeoning damage, DC 14 Dexterity save for half).  

### **Challenges to Make It Fair but Difficult**

1. **Enemy Interference**:  
   - If the players are in combat or being pursued, enemies may attempt to stop or hinder their efforts.  

2. **Multiple Steps Required**:  
   - Players must locate weak points, execute their plan, and escape within a limited timeframe.  

3. **High Stakes for Failure**:  
   - A poorly executed collapse may only partially damage the area, leaving enemies alive or scattering them to regroup.  
   - Failure to escape could result in serious harm or being trapped.  

4. **Time Pressure**:  
   - If the cavern is occupied or critical to the campaign, delay may cause the opportunity to collapse the cavern to be lost.  

### **Outcome of Success**

1. **Total Collapse**:  
   The cavern collapses entirely, trapping or killing all occupants. Survivors may be scattered and disorganized.  

2. **Partial Collapse**:  
   A partial collapse might injure or kill some occupants but leave others alive. This can lead to further encounters or confrontations.  

3. **Escape Amid Chaos**:  
   Players escape as the collapse finishes, either blending in with fleeing creatures or disappearing into the terrain.  

### **Rewarding Creativity**
Give players freedom to devise their own plans—whether through explosives, strategic use of spells, or environmental manipulation. If they fail to notice weak points, you can provide clues through environmental descriptions or NPC interactions without diminishing their sense of agency.
