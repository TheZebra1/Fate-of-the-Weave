# Beshaba
## Profile
[[faerunian-beshaba-scag|Beshaba]]
#### What They Want:
- *To spread misfortune and suffering across the world.*
- *To corrupt fate, ensuring all who thrive eventually fall.*
- *To see her sister, Tymora, undone and her concept of "good luck" eradicated.*

#### What They Fear:
- *Being forgotten, as the fear of bad luck fuels her divine power.*
- *Tymora’s influence growing so strong that mortals no longer fear misfortune.*
- *The loss of control over the chaotic forces of luck, leaving her with nothing but random destruction.*

#### What They Have Lost:
- *Her original unity with Tymora before the **Twinning of Tyche**.*
- *The ability to bring calculated misfortune—her divine essence thrives on raw, uncontrolled bad luck.*
- *The reverence of mortals who see her as a force to be appeased rather than truly worshiped.*

---

## History Summary

### **Origins & The Twinning of Tyche**
- Beshaba was born from the **divine split of Tyche**, the original goddess of fortune, when she was **corrupted by Moander**.
- The darker essence of Tyche **manifested as Beshaba**, embodying **chaos, misfortune, and disaster**.
- The lighter half, **Tymora**, became the goddess of good luck and fortune.

### **Role in Faerûn**
- Beshaba is **worshiped out of fear rather than reverence**.
- Many **superstitious individuals, sailors, and gamblers** make **offerings to appease her**, hoping to avoid her wrath.
- Her priests **spread misfortune, accidents, and curses**, believing that true chaos must be maintained.

### **Rivalry with Tymora**
- Beshaba despises her sister and seeks **to twist Tymora’s fortune into ruin**.
- Their divine influence is constantly **in opposition**, with Tymora’s luck working against Beshaba’s misfortune.
- Despite their enmity, both **must exist for luck to remain a force in the world**.

### **Current Status**
- Beshaba’s influence is strongest in **times of war, plagues, and disasters**.
- She continues to **thrive on fear**, ensuring that mortals always fear her wrath.
- Temples to her exist **not to worship her, but to ward her off**, filled with offerings meant to keep her at bay.
