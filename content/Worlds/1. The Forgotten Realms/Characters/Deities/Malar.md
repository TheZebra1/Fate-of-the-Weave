# The Origin of Lycanthropy: Malar’s Savage Gift

Long ago, before the curse of lycanthropy plagued the lands, **Malar, the Beastlord**, was a lesser deity of the hunt, revered by savage tribes and feared by civilized folk. He thrived on the chaos of predator and prey, on the primal joy of the kill. But as civilizations grew, they began to tame the wilderness, cutting forests, driving out predators, and imposing their own order on the natural world. This encroachment enraged Malar, for he saw it as a direct assault on his domain.

In his fury, Malar sought to create an army of predators to reassert his dominion over the world. He roamed the material plane in the form of a monstrous, shadowy beast, hunting mortals who dared to challenge the wild. Those he killed outright were the lucky ones; the unfortunate survivors found themselves cursed. They were transformed into savage creatures, part human and part beast, driven by an insatiable hunger for the hunt and blood.

These cursed mortals became the first lycanthropes, their forms and traits modeled after Malar’s own savage visage. **Wolves, bears, rats, boars, and tigers**—Malar’s creations reflected the diversity of predators in nature. Each type of lycanthrope became a living embodiment of his will, spreading terror and chaos wherever they roamed.

## Why the Full Moon?

Malar, being a god of chaos and savagery, is cunning but not without symbolic intent. His choice to tie lycanthropy to the full moon was deliberate and served a singular, cruel purpose:

### **A Mockery of Civilization**
Malar despised the ordered cycles of civilized life—planting, harvesting, and the structure of mortal calendars. The full moon, a recurring event that mortals could predict but could never stop, became a perfect tool for his wrath. Every full moon, Malar could remind mortals of their helplessness in the face of his savagery, unleashing chaos on their carefully ordered lives.

By making the full moon the time of transformation, Malar ensured that even the most orderly and prepared civilizations would face periodic destruction. Villages fortified against predators would crumble from within as friends and family turned into beasts, unable to resist the pull of the curse. It was his cruel way of showing that no walls, no laws, and no order could ever tame the wilderness—or escape his wrath.

---

## Selûne’s Reclamation

Selûne, the goddess of the moon, was outraged by Malar’s perversion of her domain. The moon, long a symbol of guidance, hope, and tranquility, was now feared as a harbinger of chaos and bloodshed. Selûne saw this as a direct affront, a defilement of her sacred light.

In her sorrow and fury, Selûne sought to reclaim her influence over those cursed by Malar. She blessed certain lycanthropes—those who resisted their bloodlust and sought to temper their primal instincts—with her divine grace. These individuals became good-aligned lycanthropes, such as werebears, whose transformations under the full moon were not driven by savagery but by a desire to protect others. 

Through her blessings, Selûne turned some werecreatures into guardians of the wild, defenders of balance rather than agents of chaos. This was her way of asserting that the moon’s light could still guide and inspire, even in the face of Malar’s corruption.

---

## Conclusion

Malar’s curse of lycanthropy remains a blight upon the world, a constant reminder of the Beastlord’s hatred for civilization. The full moon, once a symbol of serenity, became a mark of terror, its cycles a grim herald of transformation and chaos. Yet, through Selûne’s efforts, a glimmer of hope persists. Some lycanthropes, touched by her light, resist the curse’s pull and use their powers for good, proving that even in the darkest of nights, the moon’s light can still shine as a beacon of redemption.
