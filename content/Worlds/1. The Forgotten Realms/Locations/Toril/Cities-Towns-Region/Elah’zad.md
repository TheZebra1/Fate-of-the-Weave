**A secluded and sacred oasis, Elah'zad serves as a spiritual center for the Bedine tribes amidst the harsh Anauroch desert.**
**Social Hubs:** None (Sacred Site)

#### Notable Buildings and Persons:
- **[[House of the Moon]]:** A circular, alabaster temple dedicated to the goddess Eldath, situated on a crescent-shaped island within the [[Shadow Sea]]

#### Key Summary
- **Geography:** Elah'zad is nestled within the Hills of Scent in [[Anauroch Desert]], characterized by towering mounds encircling a vibrant blue lake, which nourishes unique flora with red leaves and metallic-hued stems.
- **Society:** While uninhabited, the oasis is a revered pilgrimage site for the nomadic Bedine tribes, who consider its waters sacred and prohibit their camels from drinking from the lake.
- **Religion:** Central to the oasis is the House of the Moon, a temple honoring Eldath, the goddess of peace and calm waters; the site is also associated with Selûne, reflecting its lunar connections.
- **Emotion:** **Serenity** – The tranquil waters and lush, colorful vegetation evoke a profound sense of peace and spiritual reverence.
- **Resources:** The oasis itself is the primary resource, offering a rare and vital source of water and spiritual sustenance in the desert; no commercial exports or imports are associated with Elah'zad.

### Currency
Not Applicable – As a sacred site, Elah'zad does not engage in commerce or trade.

### History:
- **Ancient Significance:** According to [[Bedine]] legend, the oasis was once the abode of the moon goddess Elah ([[Selûne]]) before being overtaken by [[At'ar]], the sun goddess, who transformed it into a prison for [[Eldath]] out of jealousy.
- **Cultural Gatherings:** In 1360 DR, Elah'zad served as the meeting place for sheikhs of the Bedine tribes to discuss threats such as the encroaching influence of the [[Zhentarim]].
- **Spiritual Practices:** The House of the Moon is believed to be a place where Eldath can communicate through any woman entering the temple, and where her magical items can be recharged through prayer.
