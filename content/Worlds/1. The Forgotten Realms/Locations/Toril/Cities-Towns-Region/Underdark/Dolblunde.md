## Exploring Ruins
>As you venture deeper into the abandoned ruins of Dolblunde, the air grows heavier, each breath weighted with the faint tang of ancient decay. Your torchlight flickers against the cracked stone walls, and there, carved deep into the rock, you see it—a massive set of claw marks. 

>The gouges are impossibly large, stretching nearly six feet across, their edges smooth and precise as though etched with unnatural precision. Between the gouges, faint whorls of Draconic runes glimmer faintly, pulsing with a dim, cold light. The runes spiral inward, as if drawing the eye—and the mind—into their hypnotic patterns. 

>Beneath the markings, the stone is blackened and cracked, radiating an eerie aura of necrotic energy that seems to sap the warmth from the air around you. A subtle growl seems to hum in the back of your skull, vibrating faintly through the runes before it fades into silence. Whatever made this, it was neither hurried nor careless—it feels deliberate as though it was an artistic expression or careful experiment.

## Old Temple
>In a secluded chamber off the main path, the walls are smooth and curiously intact compared to the surrounding ruins. The air here is cool, and a faint blue glow suffuses the space, emanating from intricate carvings etched into the walls.

>A massive mural dominates the far wall, depicting a serpentine dragon coiled around a starburst of light. Its form is skeletal yet majestic, its tail winding into patterns that resemble the strands of a woven net. The light of the starburst shimmers faintly, as though alive, and tiny motes of silvery energy drift lazily around the chamber, whispering faintly in an ancient, unintelligible tongue.

>Closer inspection reveals more: along the edges of the dragon’s skeletal body are smaller symbols—stars, crescent moons, and weaves of interconnected strands that pulse faintly with magical energy. At the base of the mural, carved into the stone floor, is an intricate magical circle, its runes unmistakably tied to Mystra’s teachings. The circle feels dormant, but its design is flawless, an almost impossible fusion of necromantic and arcane symbology. 

>The energy here is not hostile, but ancient and watchful. It feels as though you are being studied, each movement weighed by unseen eyes. This is not a place forgotten—it is a place remembered, and fiercely so.
