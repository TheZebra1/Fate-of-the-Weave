# **Trial of Empathy – Reliving the Rejection of Hartkiller**
## **Setup**  
As the aspirant stands upon the Throne of the First Storm, reality ripples. The chamber fades, and they are pulled into a vision—a memory carved into the soul of giantkind. They are no longer themselves. They are now one of the ancient lords of [[Ostoria]], seated among a grand council of stone in the Hall of Titans.

**The hall is vast, echoing with magic and age, its walls etched with glowing runes. Thunder rumbles in the distance. Dozens of powerful giants stand in attendance—storm, fire, frost, cloud, and more—gathered to witness Hartkiller’s claim.**

*You are seated in the Hall of Titans. The debate has begun. The fate of Hartkiller is being decided. Again.*

The aspirant is placed in the role of **[[Paramount]] [[Vilmos]]**, the progenitor or "Adam" of Giants.  

## **The Vision Begins**  
**DM reads aloud or paraphrases:**

*You feel the weight of ancient robes on your shoulders. The cold stone throne beneath you. The murmuring of voices like distant avalanches. You raise a hand, and the chamber quiets. A storm giant steps forward into the light.*

*He is young but resolute. His voice is calm, noble, and earnest.*

> **Hartkiller:** "My blood is that of the All-Father. I come not to demand loyalty, but to offer a future. We have lived too long in scattered holds and icy graves. I ask for unity—not subjugation. Let us forge something new."

>Your **Council of Giants** stand in regal clothing behind you **unimpressed** at his words and ready themselves to offer you council as needed. **All** await your **thoughts**

**Player Interjection Point 1:**  
Before the other lords respond, the player may speak as [[Vilmos]]. They may question Hartkiller, voice skepticism, or even offer early support.

## **The Chorus of Doubt**  
A series of NPC giant lords begin to object:

- A frost giant general scoffs:  
  > "He is half-mortal. His blood is thin. He speaks like a human."

- A fire giant forgemaster spits into the flame:  
  > "He has no warband. No conquest. No victories. What does he offer but pretty words?"

- A cloud giant noble murmurs to the council:  
  > "My lord the poison that even now saps your life away is because of him, the omen is alone is enough, why entertain this?"

**Player Interjection Point 2:**  
The player may respond to any of these arguments. They might defend Hartkiller, agree cautiously, or even raise their own concerns. Encourage them to step into the mindset of a tradition-bound leader.

**If the player supports Hartkiller publicly**, some NPCs take notice—there’s a visible shift in the room, though others scowl in response.

**If the player remains silent or joins the doubt**, the mood sours, and murmurs grow louder.

## **The Breaking Point**  
Hartkiller steps forward, earnest and unshaken.

> **Hartkiller:** "If none will follow me willingly, then I will go alone. But know this: the All-Father’s fire still burns, and I carry its flame, whether you choose to see it or not."

Suddenly, a lesser warlord, inflamed by pride and fear, **draws a weapon and charges**.

> "Blasphemer! You speak of unity, yet divide us with your arrogance!"

**Time slows. The player watches this unfold from within the scene.**

## **Decision Point: Save or Let Him Fall**

**The player has a split moment to act.** They can:

- **Intervene** — standing and physically shielding Hartkiller or using magic or voice to halt the attacker.
- **Hesitate or do nothing** — watching the blow land and history repeat.

## **The Vision Ends**
The chamber fades. The player stands again upon the Throne of the First Storm, but now the echoes of the past weigh heavy.

## **DM Notes: Player Impact Options**
### Likely Reaction 1: The player supports Hartkiller early and defends him at the end.
- **Outcome:** The ancestral echoes view the aspirant as someone capable of breaking the cycle. The Trial is passed with honor. The spirit of Hartkiller nods in silent thanks.

### Likely Reaction 2: The player expresses doubt or lets the attack unfold.
- **Outcome:** The trial is not failed outright—but the echoes leave them with a lesson in humility. The Crown of the First Storm may still be offered, but the aspirant must now prove redemption in a later test or decision.

This trial is not about perfection. It is about **growth, empathy, and reflection**—the chance to right a wrong not by force, but by understanding.
